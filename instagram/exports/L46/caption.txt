Algebra and inverse trigonometric integrals

Algebra can reveal a familiar inverse-trig antiderivative hidden in a quadratic denominator.
The arctangent pattern is a reciprocal of a positive square plus a constant square.

1. Scale the squared expression so the denominator becomes 4(1+u²).
2. Retain the coefficient introduced by the substitution.
3. Return to x; differentiating verifies the result for all real x.

Remember: A numerator unlike the denominator derivative does not automatically give a logarithm.
